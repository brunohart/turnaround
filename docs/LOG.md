## Day 3 — 2026-09-09 — Demand

**Shipped.** The placeholder objective is gone (ADR-006 superseded by ADR-011). A title may carry a `demand` block: expected admissions for its *first* session in each daypart on an ordinary day, a `decay` per further session in the same daypart (the second 19:00 show earns less), `weekday` multipliers by day name, a `holiday` multiplier that applies when the day's policy says `school_holiday`, and an `assumptions` list so the file says in words where its numbers came from. A title without a block is not refused: `weight` × daypart weight × the house's `assumed_admissions` (a Policy field, 100 by default) stands in, with the default decay, so every existing brief still solves and still means something. The objective is now Σ min(expected, capacity): seats *sold*, not seats offered. In the model that is one rank literal per (screen, film, daypart, rank) — the rank count is an upper bound on how many sessions of the title can start in that daypart at all — tied to the session booleans by a sum per screen, at most one holder per rank, ranks filling in order; the solver hands out ranks, and because a bigger room can only sell more, the biggest room takes rank 0 and the objective equals the sorted sum exactly. The hold penalty is now in expected admissions (the Day 2 debt), and the grid carries three figures instead of one: `admissions` (the claim), `hold_paid`, and `objective` = admissions − hold_paid. The checker was written first: `admissions()` re-counts seats sold from the brief and the sessions alone, biggest room first within each title × daypart; the `admissions` check holds the solver's claim to within a cent a session (a hand grid with no claim is reported, not failed); the `objective` check holds objective to admissions − hold_paid; the week gains `hold_paid`, which re-derives what every day after the anchor owed and fails a day that paid something else. The report: per title, seats on offer, expected admissions, and expected turned away at capacity; the sheet's by-title table gains Expected and Turned away columns and a day-total row, the meta leads with expected admissions and names the weekday and school holidays, turned away prints in rust only when non-zero, the footer states the objective in admissions and what was paid to hold; the CLI says who comes, who sits, who is turned away and which titles turned them away. `examples/demand.json` is the Regent's school-holiday week with every assumption written beside its number: the PLF event title fills the big room in prime, the family titles are front-loaded to the matinee and lifted 50–70 % by the holidays, the horror lives after 20:45 and ignores school. ## Day 2 — 2026-09-08 — The week

**Shipped.** The week as a unit. A `WeekBrief` is one house, one slate, one base policy and up to seven `DayOverride`s, each a name, a date, and only what differs: policy fields (`last_start 22:30` on Friday and Saturday, `open 11:00` on Sunday) and per-film term fields (the opening exclusive lifts on Monday; cheap Tuesday gives the family title a third show). `week.day(i)` unfolds to a plain `Brief`, so the solver and the checker never learn what a week is, and every existing term, conflict and relaxation path works unchanged per day (ADR-009). `solve_week` runs day by day into a `WeekGrid` of seven `Grid`s; an infeasible day keeps its own conflict and the rest still solve; `--relax` stays per day. The soft term: the first hold day that solves is the anchor, and every later hold day pays `hold_penalty` weighted seats (60 by default) per title whose *set of start times* differs from the anchor's — one `differs` literal per title, forced on if any anchor start is not even on offer that day, so nothing is ever forced and a title that cannot hold simply pays. `WeekGrid.held` lists the titles that kept their starts across every solved hold day. The checker (`check_week`, no solver code) re-checks every day against its unfolded brief, then the week's own claims: the grids line up with the days, and the `held` list is exactly what an independent re-read of the start sets finds; a forged claim fails. The CLI tells a week brief from a day brief by its `days` key, so `plan` / `check` / `render` / `validate` all take either.
