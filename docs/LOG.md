## Day 2 — 2026-09-08 — The week

**Shipped.** The week as a unit. A `WeekBrief` is one house, one slate, one base policy and up to seven `DayOverride`s, each a name, a date, and only what differs: policy fields (`last_start 22:30` on Friday and Saturday, `open 11:00` on Sunday) and per-film term fields (the opening exclusive lifts on Monday; cheap Tuesday gives the family title a third show). `week.day(i)` unfolds to a plain `Brief`, so the solver and the checker never learn what a week is, and every existing term, conflict and relaxation path works unchanged per day (ADR-009). `solve_week` runs day by day into a `WeekGrid` of seven `Grid`s; an infeasible day keeps its own conflict and the rest still solve; `--relax` stays per day. The soft term: the first hold day that solves is the anchor, and every later hold day pays `hold_penalty` weighted seats (60 by default) per title whose *set of start times* differs from the anchor's — one `differs` literal per title, forced on if any anchor start is not even on offer that day, so nothing is ever forced and a title that cannot hold simply pays. `WeekGrid.held` lists the titles that kept their starts across every solved hold day. The checker (`check_week`, no solver code) re-checks every day against its unfolded brief, then the week's own claims: the grids line up with the days, and the `held` list is exactly what an independent re-read of the start sets finds; a forged claim fails. The CLI tells a week brief from a day brief by its `days` key, so `plan` / `check` / `render` / `validate` all take either. The sheet gains the week view: the day grid and tables became Jinja macros shared by both sheets; the week sheet opens with the by-title table across seven days (hold days washed in navy, a *HOLDS* stamp in blue — the orange stays on the turnaround), the week's proof, then every day on its own page with a print page-break. `examples/regent-week.json` is the Regent's Thu–Wed. Hypothesis arrives: 25 random small briefs through the solver always check clean, and 300 random hand-built grids get the same verdict from the checker and an all-pairs brute-force re-read written from the brief in the test. Nine week tests besides. 36 tests in 18 s.

## Day 1 — 2026-09-07 — Infeasibility, explained

**Shipped.** Every distributor term now sits behind its own assumption literal in the model, one `TermRef` per (film, term) for `min_shows`, `max_shows`, `prime_shows` and `exclusive_screen`. On `INFEASIBLE` the solver first probes each term alone (a term that fails by itself is a conflict of size one and is reported as such), otherwise takes the unsat core from `sufficient_assumptions_for_infeasibility()` and shrinks it by deletion so nothing is blamed that the contradiction does not need. `plan` exits 2 and speaks the trade's words: *"this cannot hold on its own: The Long Voyage min_shows 6 — the house has 3 screens (1 PLF), doors 10:00 to last start 21:30 and a 17:30–20:45 prime window."* `plan --relax` drops terms from the conflict one at a time (exclusive last, lightest film first, prime before max before min) until a grid exists; the grid carries `relaxed`, the CLI prints each drop, and the sheet stamps *RELAXED* in rust beside the failing check with "4 terms given up · see proof" in the header. The checker (still no solver code, ADR-003) accepts a failure only where the grid declares it, and fails a declared relaxation that names a film or term the brief does not carry; `Report.ok` tolerates declared relaxations, `Report.clean` does not. `examples/regent-overbooked.json` (minimums doubled, Harvest given a prime guarantee) is the infeasible showcase; `run.sh` re-plans it with `--relax` when plain `plan` exits 2. Nine new tests: three constructed conflicts, each confirmed minimal by the solver itself; the relax order; the doubled Regent relaxed to a checkable grid; declared, undeclared and bogus relaxations through the checker.
