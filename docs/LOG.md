## Day 4 — 2026-09-10 — The booth's realities

**Shipped.** Five things the booth knows that the model did not. *Credits overlap:* a film's `credits_min` lets the turnaround begin that many minutes before the feature ends; the block shrinks by the same, and the room is clear at the later of feature end and turnaround end, so the next preshow never starts over the last reel (ADR-012). *Preshow by format:* `policy.preshow_by_format` gives 3D or PLF a longer preshow than the house figure (`brief.preshow_for`). *Staff:* `policy.max_concurrent_turnarounds` is how many rooms the floor can clear at once — in the model an optional interval per candidate over its turnaround under `add_cumulative` with the staff as capacity (left out when the cap is not below the screen count, since it could never bind); in the checker a count of open turnarounds at the moment each one begins. *Per-screen hours:* `screen.open` and `screen.last_start` override the house's; candidates, the redundant day-length sum and the `hours` check all read the screen's own; the slot grid stays anchored to the house's opening. *The stagger as a window:* `stagger_min` is now the window and `max_starts_per_window` the count (default 1, so 1-in-10 is what every existing brief still means); the model's sliding sum takes the count, the checker counts every window that begins at a start. ## Day 3 — 2026-09-09 — Demand

**Shipped.** The placeholder objective is gone (ADR-006 superseded by ADR-011). A title may carry a `demand` block: expected admissions for its *first* session in each daypart on an ordinary day, a `decay` per further session in the same daypart (the second 19:00 show earns less), `weekday` multipliers by day name, a `holiday` multiplier that applies when the day's policy says `school_holiday`, and an `assumptions` list so the file says in words where its numbers came from. A title without a block is not refused: `weight` × daypart weight × the house's `assumed_admissions` (a Policy field, 100 by default) stands in, with the default decay, so every existing brief still solves and still means something. The objective is now Σ min(expected, capacity): seats *sold*, not seats offered. In the model that is one rank literal per (screen, film, daypart, rank) — the rank count is an upper bound on how many sessions of the title can start in that daypart at all — tied to the session booleans by a sum per screen, at most one holder per rank, ranks filling in order; the solver hands out ranks, and because a bigger room can only sell more, the biggest room takes rank 0 and the objective equals the sorted sum exactly. The hold penalty is now in expected admissions (the Day 2 debt), and the grid carries three figures instead of one: `admissions` (the claim), `hold_paid`, and `objective` = admissions − hold_paid. The checker was written first: `admissions()` re-counts seats sold from the brief and the sessions alone, biggest room first within each title × daypart; the `admissions` check holds the solver's claim to within a cent a session (a hand grid with no claim is reported, not failed); the `objective` check holds objective to admissions − hold_paid; the week gains `hold_paid`, which re-derives what every day after the anchor owed and fails a day that paid something else. The report: per title, seats on offer, expected admissions, and expected turned away at capacity; the sheet's by-title table gains Expected and Turned away columns and a day-total row, the meta leads with expected admissions and names the weekday and school holidays, turned away prints in rust only when non-zero, the footer states the objective in admissions and what was paid to hold; the CLI says who comes, who sits, who is turned away and which titles turned them away. `examples/demand.json` is the Regent's school-holiday week with every assumption written beside its number: the PLF event title fills the big room in prime, the family titles are front-loaded to the matinee and lifted 50–70 % by the holidays, the horror lives after 20:45 and ignores school. Eight new tests: `expected` decays and scales; the weekday comes from the date; two shows in one daypart earn less than one in each; raising a title's demand moves it to the bigger room and pushes the light title's guaranteed shows to the small one; capping capacity leaves turned-away non-zero and reported in the checker, the sheet and the CLI; a forged admissions claim fails `admissions`, a forged objective fails `objective`; the hold penalty is paid in admissions and a week that claims it was not paid fails `hold_paid`; a forecast for a daypart the house does not define is refused in a sentence. The random-brief property now attaches a stated demand block to about half its titles, a random weekday and a random holiday flag, so Hypothesis proves the rank formulation agrees with the checker's sorted re-count across the space, not just on the Regent. 44 tests.

## Day 2 — 2026-09-08 — The week

**Shipped.** The week as a unit. A `WeekBrief` is one house, one slate, one base policy and up to seven `DayOverride`s, each a name, a date, and only what differs: policy fields (`last_start 22:30` on Friday and Saturday, `open 11:00` on Sunday) and per-film term fields (the opening exclusive lifts on Monday; cheap Tuesday gives the family title a third show). `week.day(i)` unfolds to a plain `Brief`, so the solver and the checker never learn what a week is, and every existing term, conflict and relaxation path works unchanged per day (ADR-009). `solve_week` runs day by day into a `WeekGrid` of seven `Grid`s; an infeasible day keeps its own conflict and the rest still solve; `--relax` stays per day. The soft term: the first hold day that solves is the anchor, and every later hold day pays `hold_penalty` weighted seats (60 by default) per title whose *set of start times* differs from the anchor's — one `differs` literal per title, forced on if any anchor start is not even on offer that day, so nothing is ever forced and a title that cannot hold simply pays. `WeekGrid.held` lists the titles that kept their starts across every solved hold day. The checker (`check_week`, no solver code) re-checks every day against its unfolded brief, then the week's own claims: the grids line up with the days, and the `held` list is exactly what an independent re-read of the start sets finds; a forged claim fails. The CLI tells a week brief from a day brief by its `days` key, so `plan` / `check` / `render` / `validate` all take either. The sheet gains the week view: the day grid and tables became Jinja macros shared by both sheets; the week sheet opens with the by-title table across seven days (hold days washed in navy, a *HOLDS* stamp in blue — the orange stays on the turnaround), the week's proof, then every day on its own page with a print page-break. `examples/regent-week.json` is the Regent's Thu–Wed. Hypothesis arrives: 25 random small briefs through the solver always check clean, and 300 random hand-built grids get the same verdict from the checker and an all-pairs brute-force re-read written from the brief in the test. ## Day 1 — 2026-09-07 — Infeasibility, explained

**Shipped.** Every distributor term now sits behind its own assumption literal in the model, one `TermRef` per (film, term) for `min_shows`, `max_shows`, `prime_shows` and `exclusive_screen`. On `INFEASIBLE` the solver first probes each term alone (a term that fails by itself is a conflict of size one and is reported as such), otherwise takes the unsat core from `sufficient_assumptions_for_infeasibility()` and shrinks it by deletion so nothing is blamed that the contradiction does not need. `plan` exits 2 and speaks the trade's words: *"this cannot hold on its own: The Long Voyage min_shows 6 — the house has 3 screens (1 PLF), doors 10:00 to last start 21:30 and a 17:30–20:45 prime window."* `plan --relax` drops terms from the conflict one at a time (exclusive last, lightest film first, prime before max before min) until a grid exists; the grid carries `relaxed`, the CLI prints each drop, and the sheet stamps *RELAXED* in rust beside the failing check with "4 terms given up · see proof" in the header. The checker (still no solver code, ADR-003) accepts a failure only where the grid declares it, and fails a declared relaxation that names a film or term the brief does not carry; `Report.ok` tolerates declared relaxations, `Report.clean` does not. `examples/regent-overbooked.json` (minimums doubled, Harvest given a prime guarantee) is the infeasible showcase; `run.sh` re-plans it with `--relax` when plain `plan` exits 2. Nine new tests: three constructed conflicts, each confirmed minimal by the solver itself; the relax order; the doubled Regent relaxed to a checkable grid; declared, undeclared and bogus relaxations through the checker.
